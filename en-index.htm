A NEW CHAPTER FOR SIMPLY SCANDINAVIA

We are delighted to announce that Simply Scandinavia will become part of Scanglas Holdings on 1 January 2027. Scanglas Holdings is a unique family-owned company that has, since 1990, developed its range of hand-blown replicas of historical glassware, spanning the earliest days of glassblowing through to the 19th century.

Over the years, Scanglas has expanded its range of handcrafted products. In addition to its historical collections, it now also serves the home and architectural conservation sector, hotels and restaurants, television productions and more. Scanglas has retailers in 20 countries around the world.

Simply Scandinavia has built its identity around porcelain inspired by historical patterns and Scandinavian cultural heritage, with customers including museums, interior design shops and florists.

Becoming part of Scanglas Holdings marks the beginning of an exciting new chapter for Simply Scandinavia. Scanglas shares our passion for history, craftsmanship and timeless design, creating new opportunities to develop the brand and its collections further. We look forward to continuing to serve our existing customers and partners while introducing Simply Scandinavia to new markets and customers around the world.

We would also like to take this opportunity to thank our customers and partners for the trust you have placed in us over the years. Business will continue as usual until 31 December 2026. If you have any questions, please do not hesitate to contact us.

PAGOD is a classic Chinese pattern that has been copied by many European factories, even by Swedish companies. The pattern is available on several different pots.

Simply Scandinavia produces the most genuine reproductions of Chinese porcelain, planters, vases, bowls and plates of traditional design. East Indian is the name of the porcelain patterns which were exported to Europe originating in the traditional patterns that the Chinese themselves appreciated. Export porcelain on the other hand is considered to be the designs and patterns that the European trade companies ordered in China based on Western drawings, meticulously copied by Chinese craftsmen. Each family of means ordered dinner services with their own family crest.

Pagodas are Buddhist buidings for housing holy manuscripts or relics. In China they developed into tall towers with several floors.

The main event during the coronation festivities was a so-called Carousel, a performed and predetermined battle between different peoples. The court painter Ehrenstral was tasked with creating the magnificent costumes and decor, and later to perpetuate the events in a richly illustrated book. The King won the battle, naturally!

The richest family in Sweden in the 18th century was the Grills. The family had immigrated from Italy by way of the Netherlands and Germany. In Sweden they built up their great fortune through interests in iron mills, ship trade, banking etc.
